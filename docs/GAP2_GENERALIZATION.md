# Gap 2: generalization to larger glasses and different quench histories

## Question and scope

Can the three existing N=256-trained D2 models place a requested defect in larger glasses and glasses prepared at different cooling rates, without retraining, retuning guidance, or redefining a defect? This study tests zero-shot transfer within the same 2D KA potential, density and composition. It does not establish transfer to a different material, potential or dimension.

## Fixed evaluation matrix

| Atoms | Cooling rate (temperature drop per LJ time) | Role |
|---:|---:|---|
| 256 | 0.01 | Fresh in-distribution control |
| 1024 | 0.01 | Size shift only |
| 256 | 0.001 | Slower cooling only |
| 256 | 0.1 | Faster cooling only |
| 1024 | 0.001 | Combined size and slower-history shift; main new result |
| 1024 | 0.1 | Combined size and faster-history shift |

Each cell uses 100 independently generated host glasses, followed by 256 saved uniform-site requests cycled over those hosts. Dataset configurations live in `configs/data/gap2/`. The evaluation seed ranges are disjoint from the original training/validation/test ranges and from one another. Hosts must be regenerated by MD, never tiled from N=256 structures. Two sites in the same glass are not independent hosts.

The size increase is fourfold in atom count and twofold in box length, at density 1.2. Atom-count rounding gives 666 A and 358 B atoms at N=1024, versus 166 A and 90 B at N=256; report the realized fractions. Cooling rates are 10x slower/faster than training preparation. Melt temperature, melt duration, endpoint temperature, density, thermostat and minimization settings remain fixed. Report per-condition energy, defect prevalence, order, residual forces and rejection counts; applying a common protocol does not by itself prove distinct structural ensembles.

The primary combined-shift condition is fixed before evaluating the models. Do not choose whichever new condition happens to look strongest.

## Frozen models and evaluation

- Use the original three trained checkpoints (seed0, seed1 and seed2) retained in `scripts/repeat_credibility.py`. These are independent training seeds, not three sampling repetitions of one model. Report each seed, their mean and spread. The trained weights stay fixed across all six cells.
- Freeze the original training-derived defect thresholds in `data/ka2d_256/meta.json`. Do not recalibrate on the new test glasses. Training patch libraries for hand insertion must also remain from the original training data.
- Freeze the gap-1 D2 setting: guidance 2, radius 6, sigma_max 1, 100 noisy plus 100 final sampling steps. Radius 6 remains a physical length; do not scale it with box size. At N=1024 the regenerated region occupies a smaller fraction of the box, which is part of this transfer test.
- Save requests once per condition. The three models, the seed0 null-label sham, and hand insertion must use exactly the same hosts and target sites. Use the same sampling/noise rule across the three models and sham within a condition. Different atom counts preclude identical full noise arrays across system sizes.
- Keep the initially-nonD2 subset as the primary placement denominator; report all-request results too. Also report an unedited-host control so naturally present defects are not counted as new creations.
- Relax every output with all atoms free and the same force tolerance. Include failed placements and nonconverged outputs in request accounting. Report convergence separately; do not silently discard failures or change relaxation limits on one condition after seeing success.
- GPU batches must be benchmarked at N=1024 rather than assuming N=256 memory use. Changing batches changes the noise assignment; fix and record batch sizes before the main run. Existing geometric/potential operations include dense pair calculations, so four times the atoms can cost much more than four times the memory or runtime.

## Outcomes and uncertainty

Primary endpoint: newly placed, relaxed D2 success at the requested site in the N=1024, slower-cooling condition, for all three trained seeds, compared with matched insertion and sham controls. Report the change relative to fresh N=256 standard-history performance. Do not define generalization merely as observing a nonzero number of successes.

Report each seed separately with whole-host bootstrap intervals. Paired method comparisons resample the same hosts and preserve matched requests. Comparisons between independently generated conditions resample each condition's hosts separately. Do not treat 256 requests or three velocity realizations as independent glasses, or treat the three checkpoints as hundreds of independent training runs. Three training seeds give a limited estimate of training variability.

Secondary endpoints: energy and structural mismatch around successful placements against natural defects from the same size/history, count-matched natural-versus-natural references, outside-radius host displacement, and runtime/convergence. If a condition has too few natural D2 reference hosts, report insufficient precision rather than changing the detector or expanding the reference after looking at the outcome. A fixed subset of original training natural environments can additionally distinguish a preparation-induced shift from an editing-induced mismatch, but must be labelled as a different comparison.

The gap-1 MD results do not establish thermal retention on these new conditions. Any generalized thermal-persistence claim requires new MD. Placement generalization and physical-credibility generalization must be distinguished.

## Current implementation and execution

The six production dataset configurations and the checkpoint/threshold manifest in `configs/eval/gap2_generalization.json` are ready. The existing three checkpoints supply the repeated-training-seed evaluation; new training is not needed to test zero-shot transfer. If adaptation is later investigated, report it as a separate experiment with its own independent training seeds. For example:

```bash
export LD_LIBRARY_PATH="$PWD/.venv/lib${LD_LIBRARY_PATH:+:$LD_LIBRARY_PATH}"
.venv/bin/python scripts/make_dataset.py \
  --config configs/data/gap2/ka2d_1024_slow.yaml
```

Run the other five configurations separately. `make_dataset.py` generates a complete split before saving; it does not resume individual unfinished hosts, so budget for a complete dataset job.

Before that larger expense, `scripts/benchmark_gap2.py` generates one host per condition, checks the existing geometric/force quality gates, and records wall time. It uses benchmark seeds outside the evaluation ranges and caches each completed condition:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/benchmark_gap2.py \
  --out results/gap2_feasibility_20261018 --workers 4
```

A separate `model_forward_smoke.json` records one N=1024 forward pass for each of the three retained models. These checks verify compatible tensor shapes, finite outputs, and zero displacement of pinned atoms; they do not run the diffusion sampler, relaxation or placement detector.

This benchmark establishes host-generation feasibility only. It does not run the trained models, estimate generalization, prove all 100 hosts will pass, or reliably predict tail runtime from a single glass. The production generator additionally applies its pre-existing within-condition energy-outlier gate and replaces rejected seeds; retain rejection logs and avoid comparing only post-filter energy means.

## Kaggle notebook: primary placement-transfer evaluation

Use [`notebooks/kaggle_gap2_generalization.ipynb`](../notebooks/kaggle_gap2_generalization.ipynb), with GPU T4 ×2, Internet enabled, and access to the existing `GITHUB_TOKEN` secret. Run All. One GPU is supported. The gap-1 notebook does not run this matrix.

The driver `scripts/run_gap2.py` implements resumable host generation, fixed matched requests, evaluation of three frozen checkpoints and a seed0 sham, original-training-library insertion, and host-aware placement analysis. The notebook runs all six conditions, starting with the fresh N=256 control and the primary N=1024 slow-history condition. All models and insertion use the same saved, nearest-B-snapped target in each request.

Host generation checkpoints every candidate. Candidates use seeds `seed0 + slot + 1000*attempt`, with at most 10 attempts per slot, excluding the reserved benchmark seeds. Original quality gates and the within-condition energy-outlier rule apply, with quality/energy rejections retained in the protocol. If the fixed attempt budget is exhausted, the job stops rather than silently changing the gates.

Batch size 4 is fixed for sampling and insertion; completed batches are immutable and atomically written. SHA256 fingerprints cover requests, the study manifest, trained checkpoints and the baseline patch library. Nonfinite generation and unconverged relaxation are counted as failed requests, with raw numerical/convergence diagnostics retained. They are not silently dropped. The relaxed coordinates of a numerically failed generation are only placeholders for file-shape consistency; its `finite`, `converged` and `success` fields identify the failure.

Each session has an 11-hour execution budget. Valid candidates, batches and partial reports are pushed every five minutes, and again at stage boundaries and shutdown. A fresh session restores the most recent incomplete `results/kaggle_gap2_generalization_*` run and checks out its recorded evaluation-code revision. Run All again to continue. Multiple sessions should be expected; full GPU sampling runtime and peak memory have not been benchmarked in this cloud environment. The initial checkpoint push checks GitHub write access before expensive generation.

Outputs under `study/` include per-condition host protocols and raw candidates, `requests.npz`, per-method `samples.npz`, `placement_summary.csv`, `paired_contrasts.csv`, and `study_status.json`. The summary includes initially-nonD2 and all-request denominators, all three training seeds separately, and their mean. Its bootstrap intervals resample complete hosts and condition on the three fixed trained models; they are not population intervals over arbitrary training seeds. Paired contrasts preserve host/request matching. The seed0 sham is a same-checkpoint control for seed0; comparisons of seed1/seed2 against that sham also change checkpoint weights. A degenerate zero-event bootstrap interval is not proof of zero underlying probability.

The notebook reports convergence and time per request. Reported time includes generation and relaxation; cached-batch times are the original measured durations. This first notebook addresses primary placement generalization. Successful-only structural comparisons against same-condition natural references, shell displacement, independent-condition shift intervals, and finite-temperature retention are additional analyses and are not automatically completed by a `complete` placement-study status. Production results have not been measured merely because the notebook exists.
